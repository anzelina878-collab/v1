# Cosmos cinematic stock — active revision 2

This supersedes the diagram-heavy V1 approach for the space channel. The user's latest visual instructions take precedence over templates for other channels in the linked repository.

## Visual priorities

- Full-frame moving space footage is the visual foundation. Preserve relevant stock video from the earlier edit and add a much larger varied library.
- Use beautiful, spatially readable imagery: textured stars, planets with atmospheric rims, nebulae, galactic disks, deep-field flythroughs, telescope observations, and appropriate archive footage.
- Explanatory visualizations must have visible three-dimensional depth, perspective, textured objects, lighting, and coherent camera motion. Prefer vetted NASA/ESA/ESO scientific 3D footage over an improvised drawing.
- Do not use 2D dot fields, flat charts, infographic cards, timelines, progress bars, numbered section strips, developer notes, HUD decoration, or persistent chapter headers.
- A flythrough is not evidence that cosmic expansion is tearing apart a bound object. Do not illustrate ordinary expansion with exploding planets. Match speculative sequences specifically to the narrated conditional scenario.

## Typography

- Default is a clean picture with no overlay. Add words only when identifying an object, communicating a key quantity, or distinguishing a hypothetical scenario matters to the viewer.
- Use a short German name or number, generous margins, a restrained shadow, and a brief smooth fade. Avoid long scientific caveats and production comments in the picture.
- Examples: “Roter Riese”, “Weißer Zwerg”, “Big Rip · hypothetisch”, “In etwa 5 Milliarden Jahren”. Do not show section numbers or “keine Maßstabstreue” boilerplate throughout the film.
- Integrate labels near the relevant 3D object, without covering its important features. Never accumulate overlapping labels. Full subtitles remain optional and separate.
- Put source credits and detailed caveats in the source register and publication description, not in permanent technical overlays.

## Editing

- Read the script and align to the original supplied narration. Keep the original voice, tempo, and content.
- Shape shots around spoken ideas and camera motion. Use shorter 3–6 second plans for the opening and an organic mix of approximately 8–20 second plans in explanations; these are editorial ranges, not a fixed grid.
- Alternate wide context, medium views, close details, archive footage, and spatial 3D explanations. Do not fill a long passage with the same short loop.
- A stock clip must support the actual sentence. Avoid unrelated labs, keyboards, finance graphics, and generic technology as filler.
- Inspect each selected source range for baked-in English captions, title cards, logos, interviews, black frames, and abrupt endings. Record clean in/out points.
- Preserve existing releases until the revision is accepted. Do not alter unrelated channel templates or old episodes.

## Quality and delivery

- Target 1920×1080, 25 fps, high-quality H.264/AAC. Prefer native Full HD or 4K source media. Disclose any genuinely lower-resolution scientific archive segments; upscaling does not create native detail.
- Keep the original narration and optional SRT. Validate frame-accurate coverage of the full audio, inspect representative frames and all typography, and check the final video/audio/subtitle streams.
- Keep a reproducible source manifest with exact download URLs, attribution, selected ranges, and media resolution.
- Use the user's cloud workflow. Do not substitute temporary public download hosting for the finished video. A GitHub repository is not evidence of an authenticated or running Colab session; prepare a real notebook and describe what has and has not actually executed.
