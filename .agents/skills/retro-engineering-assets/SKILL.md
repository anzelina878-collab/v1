# Retro Engineering: visuals

Apply only to retro-engineering. Read AGENTS.md, the merged profile, current script, research, and episode scope.

Read the [current video production rules](<../../../Данные проекта/research/current_production_rules.md>): search before final scene alignment, meaningful sequences, orientation within one device, and verification of narration-to-image correspondence.

Use plan.md to identify the hook's visual mystery and each block's discoveries. For a mechanism, use: overall view → relevant component → movement/flow/force → constraint → test or result. For every key explanation, record in plan.md what new knowledge the image provides. Show defects and tradeoffs through observable results: for example, a noise band/image change during adjustment, followed by the component that explains it. Select circuit boards, chips, and third-party test rigs only for a specific function or an analogy identified in narration. Returning to an overall view is allowed with a new explanatory focus; reusing it instead of the required component does not meet the visual need.

For each meaningful time window, search for video first, then photographs, then generation. A detailed query describes the device/model, specific component, action, viewpoint, and era. Start with broad Google results and video search without site:, then explore discovered archives, museums, creators' pages, YouTube, and stock libraries. Read the [search and selection procedure](references/search.md). Before switching to photos or generation, record which video candidates were checked and why they fail. Do not replace precise searching with a generic topic-name query.

Build episodes about operating or opened devices predominantly from real video, measuring its share in seconds. The first 3 seconds show expressive real action, not a photo montage. For human context, search for use, maintenance, wearing, and reactions only where they explain the exact phrase; clinical stock footage does not identify its actor as an implant patient.

Track original video segments by original source and interval: repeated and overlapping ranges are prohibited even after changing crop, color, speed, or filename. Check nonoverlapping seconds for repeated actions with no new meaning as well. After bounded diagnosis of an unavailable download, find another accessible source showing the same action while preserving model accuracy and usage rights; do not stop the whole episode or hand the search back to the user. A careful crop of a unique shot is allowed if it preserves the explanatory component.

For each finished video, track original photographs: one authentic photograph may appear in only one shot, regardless of filename, crop, color, zoom, or pan. Before rendering, detect duplicates using original URL/asset ID, hash, and visual similarity, then inspect the contact sheet. If coverage is insufficient, expand targeted searches for new real videos and photos instead of making multiple edits of one photograph. If a thorough search finds no image for a necessary scene, one **rare illustrative reconstruction** with archival-photo texture is allowed; verify technical details, record generation in manifest/search_log, and do not present it as authentic historical footage. Do not create reconstruction series to fill time. Under the user's clarification of 9 October 2026, do not add a public “reconstruction” label to the video, narration, cover, or description; retain internal provenance. See the current channel rules for the full criterion.

Whenever narration directly mentions a specific person, show that actual person in sync with the phrase: real video, a verified portrait, or an authentic group photo where they are unambiguously recognizable. Verify identity and source, and age/period where possible. A random portrait, actor, different engineer, or general device shot cannot replace the named person. For repeated mentions, find another unique photo or video shot of the same person; the photograph reuse ban still applies. If no verified image is found, continue targeted searching and retain an unresolved visual gap; do not invent their appearance or mark the shot ready. Save the depicted person's name and identification source in manifest; link the exact mention and timestamp in timeline.

Bind each shot to the exact spoken phrase by timestamps, not just a topic or paragraph. Do not distribute paragraph-relevant images into equal segments without semantic synchronization. For each timeline shot, record the exact text in its time window and what the viewer should see; copying a full paragraph into scene_text does not demonstrate correspondence. Show the person when named, the relevant component when described, and visible action, property, or precise object illustration when discussed. For example, “the assembly was potted in resin” requires the corresponding assembly and encapsulation; a patient portrait from the same paragraph is unsuitable. Check each window before rendering and the actual narration/image pairing at subject changes after rendering.

Choose expressive action: transport, rotation, loading, force transmission, testing, or results. Alternate overall views, component macros, viewpoints, and observable effects; each new shot adds information. Inspect the source video and its edits; select continuous segments using episode effective_settings: assets.default_shot_duration_seconds for long videos (default 3–7 seconds), shorts.shot_duration_range_seconds for Shorts (default 2–3 seconds), without embedded cuts, captions, or other people's speech. Verify model, era, rights, and the shot's exact relation to the phrase. In a short test, use each source once. Authentic documents are allowed when required by the story, not as filler.

Do not draw explanatory infographics, diagrams, charts, or text cards for the shared visual track. The current channel profile is Ukrainian only: explanation comes through Ukrainian narration and footage of the device working. Avoid burned-in language-specific captions. Generate a suitable illustrative image only after unsuccessful searches for real video and photos; archival-photo styling is allowed, but presenting it as an authentic archival document is not. Localized thumbnails and metadata remain separate stages.

Apply the channel's video.color_treatment: muted saturation and a light warm archival tone. Pass it to timeline.color_treatment for native FFmpeg; a setting in settings.json alone does not apply a filter. Check that details, light surfaces, and original document labels remain legible. Do not add shake, flicker, heavy grain, or dirt. Animate photos with restrained subpixel motion. Link shots to questions and explanations in timeline; preserve provenance, terms, and applied grading in manifest.

General format and mechanics: [retro-engineering-assets](references/technical-workflow.md). Save working results in Производство/episodes/<selected-episode>; do not use Bible-channel topics, sources, or assignments. Follow the current request for languages and file counts.

One failed query, convenience, speed, viewpoint variety, or episode duration is insufficient justification. Do not run generation series to fill runtime. For each exception, record the specific missing shot, checked sources, and rejection reasons. Previously generated images are not automatically selected.
