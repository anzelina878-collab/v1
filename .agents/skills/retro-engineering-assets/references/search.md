# Finding expressive mechanism footage

During draft planning, before final scene alignment for a requested video, find real anchor materials for the hook and key actions; verify their content, accessibility, and usage terms. After checking the supplied narration, divide the script into observable actions using the official timestamps. Read the [current production rules](<../../../../Данные проекта/research/current_production_rules.md>). For every time window, record the object, required component, its action, and what the viewer should see. Build searches around this brief; one “VHS” query for an entire episode is insufficient.

Query formula: device/model + component + action + viewpoint + era/context. Start with broad Google and Google Videos searches in English and Russian, then refine within a suitable discovered archive, creator's site, or YouTube. Do not restrict initial results to stock domains.

Different VHS needs require different queries:

- `VHS cassette reels slowly turning playback close up footage` — tape and reels during playback.
- `VHS VCR spinning video head drum magnetic tape macro close up video` — a fast drum beside slow tape.
- `VHS cassette loading guide rollers pull tape wrap around head drum mechanism video` — guides pulling out and threading tape.
- `VHS capstan pinch roller tape transport running mechanism close up video` — the precision tape-transport assembly.
- `VHS tracking adjustment CRT horizontal noise bands playback video` — observable image distortion; do not substitute film or random digital glitches.
- `1980s VHS VCR controller circuit board repair electronics close up footage` — electronics of the specific device type.

Adapt queries to the next topic rather than repeating VHS searches for every episode. For weak results, change component synonyms, language, model, or viewpoint. Record attempts and results; a query list alone is not a completed search.

Inspect suitable videos first. Check author, source, and terms on the page; view several frames around the selected range and actual movement. Choose continuous action, a visible relevant component, steady camera, and sufficient detail. Do not substitute another creator's intro, explanatory caption, long presenter monologue, or dark segment for the required action. For short sources, verify sufficient duration without looping: default 3–7 seconds for long videos, 2–3 for Shorts, with episode limits from effective_settings. Download only the selected permitted file/segment, not an entire channel. After bounded diagnosis of a failed download, find an authorized archival copy or another source showing the component/action; continue independent stages and do not hand searching back to the user. Permission for one recording does not extend to a replacement.

The standard YouTube license does not authorize reuse. Verify Creative Commons on the specific recording and its provenance; an archival copy with confirmed authorship and license may be preferable. Do not buy stock or ask permission for the first questionable asset; search for an alternative within scope.

After several meaningful video-search variations, use an accurate photograph. If that also fails, use illustrative GPT generation under the general skill. Record reasons for rejecting video/photos in search_log for each transition. Do not create text infographics or diagrams for the multilingual master video.

Before concluding that footage is insufficient, expand the search through component/action synonyms, models/manufacturers, languages, museums, patent archives, and available videos from another angle. Record specific inspected candidates and rejection reasons, not only query lists. Do not return an already used photograph to the edit under a new name or crop. For a truly missing necessary scene, a single generative reconstruction with archival aesthetics is allowed after verifying the device against real sources and recording provenance in internal manifest/search_log, without a public “reconstruction” label in video, narration, cover, or description. Do not use this frequently or to fill runtime.

In search_log, save scene_text, query, engine, inspected URLs, suitability, rights, accessibility, decision, and selected range. In manifest, save original author/URL, role and era, license, permission/attribution, hash, local path, and processing. Sepia does not turn a modern demonstration of a vintage device into period archival footage.

Before editing, create a contact sheet and compare every shot with its exact phrase and intended viewer understanding. Check action sequences, shot-size changes, no exact repeats, and no burned-in text. Unique files do not guarantee meaningful variety; overall views and chip markings do not explain a process. Apply current channel rules for source spacing and the exception for sequential nonoverlapping actions. From the finished timeline, calculate video share by duration and photo/generated-image counts; if photos dominate, review justifications for rejecting video. A filter does not fix weak or inaccurate material.
Check each photograph's provenance as well: the same original, even cropped/color-corrected, cannot occupy two shots in one video. Check URLs/asset IDs, hashes, and visually similar images; record repeat count in quality_report.md and remove repeats before final rendering.

One failed query, convenience, speed, viewpoint variety, or episode length is insufficient justification. Do not run generation series to fill runtime. For each exception, record the specific missing shot, checked sources, and rejection reasons. Previously generated images are not automatically selected.

Track video intervals across original sources and their copies; repeated and overlapping intervals remain prohibited after crop, speed, or color changes. Different seconds showing the same action without a new explanation do not create variety. For human-context shots, check that narration does not attribute an implant or diagnosis to a stock actor.
