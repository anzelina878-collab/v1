# Engineering mystery: writer's guide

## One question per episode

Extract the main contradiction viewers want resolved from the topic entry. Examples: why heat a refrigerator; why a storage device needs air; how water lifts a ship. Verify the premise against sources before turning it into a hook. Surprise comes from the actual mechanism and the era's constraints.

Working structure: oddity → question → problem → engineering solution → new problem → resolution → unexpected verified fact. Do not impose failure on a successful technology, catastrophe on an ordinary test, or revival on a project that never resumed.

## Structure and length

Apply the [current PASTWORKS rules](<../../../../Данные проекта/research/current_production_rules.md>). The 20–30-minute range and roughly 25-minute target guide length without imposing a duration for every section. The previous structure with a first solution at minutes 3–8 and separate long resolution blocks is replaced by an early answer and compact ending.

The first 20 seconds introduce a specific contradiction; within 20–30 seconds, viewers see it in action. In the first minute, provide an initial answer or observable evidence and the next question. Introduce historical context when needed to understand engineering choices. Alternate solution, limitation, and testing in the main section while maintaining causality. End with the answer and one strong verified fact, without recapping every chapter.

Before final scene alignment, verify found visual evidence for key explanations. Allocate length by new knowledge, actions, and tests; trim repeated definitions, identical caveats, and intermediate conclusions. Do not pad to a character target. Record reasons for substantive duration deviations in plan.md; an explicit requested runtime takes precedence. Clean script.txt excludes timestamps and section headings.

Use the measured pace of the channel's own voice; if uncalibrated, use the merged profile's working estimate and check against the supplied recording. Hour-long Polish-story norms do not apply here.

## Small mysteries and mechanism explanation

Build a 20–30-minute analysis from 6–10 logical blocks to maintain interest throughout. Each block has its own arc: question → observation or test → explanation → small climax. That climax provides a meaningful answer or discovery: unexpected component action, rejection of an obvious theory, test result, or engineering tradeoff. Derive a new question from the answer to open the next block naturally; the final block resolves the main episode question. Do not create an artificial mystery after a completed resolution.

For each block in plan.md, record its question, new knowledge, small climax, and transition question; for the last, record the final answer. Determine block lengths by meaning and complexity rather than equal durations. Discoveries within a block sustain pacing without requiring a new block each time.

Roughly every 1–2 minutes, offer a meaningful discovery: a material constraint, unusual component, unexpected test result, operating condition, or cost of simplification. In plan.md, record the transition question, answering fact, source, and suitable visual material. Density depends on explanation complexity; do not insert artificial mysteries by stopwatch.

Explanation cycle: what must be done → why the usual method fails → what changed → how it works → what it costs. Introduce a term after explaining its function. Movement of energy, signal, matter, or force matters more than a list of every part. An analogy must illustrate a specific property, then identify where it stops working.

Derive turns from documents and the device itself. Do not repeat “but another problem appeared” at every transition. After a complex explanation, briefly reinforce its consequence and move on. Do not conceal a simple answer until the ending merely to delay it: reveal the basic principle early, then explore implementation complexity.

## Voice and packaging

Use a calm documentary narrator, curious without shouting. Simple sentences, concrete actions, and occasional numbers with their significance explained. Engineers' stories belong where they affect a choice, test, or result. Do not invent dialogue, thoughts, or scenes placing the narrator at events.

Propose several title angles: strange effect, unusual solution, difficult tradeoff. The object is recognizable, but the model name need not lead. The thumbnail shows one unusual detail; the hook begins explaining the promised question. Keep CTAs brief and appropriate; they do not replace a meaningful ending.

## Facts and visual evidence

For significant claims, save the source and applicable page/figure in research_notes.md. Distinguish documented fact, promotional promise, measurement, and your own explanation. Project cancellations often have several causes: state confirmed ones and preserve uncertainty.

Verify variants, unit systems, measurement conditions, and dates. Distinguish plans from prototypes and prototypes from production. Do not turn promotional “first” into a world record without clarifying the criterion. Describe medical equipment historically rather than making the episode a self-treatment guide.

Prepare an object/component list with functions for asset selection. Prefer material showing the causal chain: overall view → component → operation → limitation → result. For each explanation, identify observable component action and a detailed video search query. Do not plan original infographics or language-specific cards for the shared master visuals; verified drawings may serve as research sources.

## Text readiness

The main question is answered; a long script has 6–10 logical blocks, each with a small climax, with transitions developing a new question; every block adds knowledge; the technical chain can be retold in simple words; hook, title, and ending agree. Length supports the requested duration without repetition. script.txt contains only spoken Ukrainian text, expanded abbreviations, and naturally written units. Links, editing directions, and research caveats belong in working files; necessary substantive qualifications belong in narration itself.
