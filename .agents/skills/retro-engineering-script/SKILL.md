# Retro Engineering: scripts

Apply to scripts and topic selection for retro-engineering. Read its AGENTS.md and merged settings. For a script, read the [engineering mystery guide](references/storytelling.md) and [current video production rules](<../../../Данные проекта/research/current_production_rules.md>). Use a number from Данные проекта/topics/topics.txt or the user's explicit topic; the catalog has 50 permanent numbers. Formulate one question about a machine, technology, or challenge in a specific historical period.

For topic requests, interleave industries: infrastructure → recording/sound → computing → transport → instruments and other fields. Vary scale and operating principles; do not create long runs of aircraft, refrigerators, or one mechanism family. Each entry needs a permanent number, intriguing working title, field, research angle, and initial primary source. An explicit request for an industry series takes precedence. Do not renumber existing entries. Selecting topics does not start every script.

Research primary sources: manuals, drawings, patents, test reports, contemporary technical literature, and manufacturer/museum archives. Verify model/variant, developers, dates, purpose, construction, and claimed versus measured specifications. A patent confirms a described idea; it does not itself prove a successful device or mass production. Distinguish design, prototype, testing, and operation.

In research_notes.md, save URLs, pages/figures, and links between significant claims and sources; list uncertainties separately. A topic entry's source is a starting point, not finished research. Give numbers with units and measurement conditions; do not mix variants. In plan.md, record the main question, hook, historical problem, mechanism's causal chain, limitations, tests, conclusion, and planned length for the specific voice. Divide a 20–30-minute script into 6–10 logical blocks with small climaxes and transitions through new questions to sustain interest throughout; record each block's structure using the [writer's guide](references/storytelling.md). For meaningful discoveries roughly every 1–2 minutes, identify new knowledge, its source, and visual material.

Before finalizing the text, verify visual feasibility of the hook and key explanations: specific found candidates, visible action, result, and limitations. For video requests, do this search before final scene alignment; for script-only requests, a plan and links suffice without full asset production. Provide the first clear answer or observable evidence within the first minute, then develop it through further constraints. Every block adds knowledge; trim repeated caveats and multiple conclusions without losing material qualifications.

Before finalizing the script, list every specific person mentioned in narration and find a verified image of each. Every mention needs a shot of that actual person; a repeated mention requires another unique portrait or video shot. For script-only requests, save verified links and a display plan without starting production.

Build the Ukrainian script as an engineering mystery: oddity → question → problem → engineering solution → new problem → resolution → unexpected verified fact. The first 20 seconds reveal a specific contradiction and promise an explanation; context follows. New turns arise from real constraints and tests. Explain component operation as a causal chain accessible to a nonspecialist. Include details and formulas when they clarify a choice. Do not invent engineers' conversations, crises, or the revival of a discontinued technology.

Use calm, curious documentary narration accessible without technical training. Do not delay answers artificially or repeat transition phrases. script.txt contains only spoken Ukrainian text, with units and abbreviations suitable for narration. Normal duration is 20–30 minutes, targeting 25 when material supports it; the requested duration takes precedence. Do not pad with repetition: justify substantive deviations in plan.md under the current rules. Do not automatically expand a finished short text.

Outputs: research_notes.md, plan.md, script.txt, and a verified list of models/terms for asset selection. For a standalone script request, record requested_stage="script", outputs.script="script.txt", and scope.script_language; after verification, delivery.py exports it as LANG/сценарий.txt. General cleanup retains research_notes.md as a record of sources, pages, claim links, and uncertainties; working plan.md is removed. A script-only request does not start synthesis or visual generation.
